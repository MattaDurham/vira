What this experiment measures

The skeleton displays the saved transforms of 29 connected physical bodies. The two cameras observe the same state. The diagram points to actual axial bone locations; a head–tail angle describes shape, while yaw describes the head’s orientation in the world. A large bend alone does not establish a completed U-turn.

The original test applied water resistance before the joint motors. The distributed-bend tests solve active motor impulses, resistance and frozen anchor velocity constraints together, then advance the body with Rapier. The active controller supplies energy. The optional 0.16-radian joint ranges and muscle occupancy are authored choices, rather than measured koi cartilage or muscle activation.

Water resistance uses 708 rigid body and caudal surface patches with quadratic normal and tangential drag. It omits added fluid mass, circulation, wakes, paired-fin propulsion, buoyancy and body self-contact. The 13 cm control scales geometry, mass, inertia and authored active capacity together. It uses a bluegill’s coarse relative body-shape sequence and initial velocity, with its head orientation and travel left free. This is a comparison across species, with no claim of dynamic similarity.

The selected run, source hashes, units and generation commands are recorded in the data manifest. The build scripts preserve the distinction between the reference motion and the physics result.

Evidence behind the experiment

Watch, measure, compare.

  • Clark and Tytell (2026): bluegill turning · Original fast-turn video · Versioned measurements. Regional angles inform the shape cue; they do not calibrate koi mechanics.
  • Wakeling and Johnston (1999): isolated carp muscle force. Maximum measured active stress informs an approximate geometric capacity envelope; routine activation and muscle occupancy remain authored.
  • Piñeirua, Godoy-Diana and Thiria (2015): resistive and reactive propulsion. This reduced test includes the resistive contribution.
  • Pinned Rapier angular motors and joint ranges. Numerical constraints are distinct from anatomical measurements.
